Turn your CDs into MP3s, named properly.

Put in a disc. Ripple copies the tracks to MP3 files on your Mac, finds the album, the song titles, and the cover, and names every file after its song.

Three steps

No setup screens and no file name templates to learn.

  1. Insert a disc

    Plug in a CD drive and insert an audio disc. Ripple shows its tracks right away.

  2. Check the details

    Ripple looks up the album, the artist, the track titles, and the cover. Fix anything that looks wrong, or pick a different match.

  3. Rip

    Press Rip. Your MP3 files land in the folder you chose, in Artist/Album folders, named 01 - Song Title.mp3.

What Ripple does

One window, a few good defaults.

Finds the album for you

Looks the disc up on MusicBrainz, a free community music database, and fetches the cover picture from the Cover Art Archive.

Names files by song title

Every file is named after its song. Characters that Macs do not allow in file names are swapped out, so nothing fails halfway.

Easy to correct

Edit the album, the artist, and any track title. Skip tracks you do not want. Drop your own picture on the cover.

Handles tricky discs

Several albums match one disc? You choose. Two-disc sets go into Disc 1 and Disc 2 folders. Compilations put the artist in each file name.

Stop any time

Cancel in the middle of a rip. The finished tracks stay, and no half-written file is left behind.

Private

No account and no tracking. The only thing Ripple sends over the internet is the album and cover lookup, and ripping works without it.

When a disc matches more than one album, you choose.

Reissues and special editions share the same disc. Ripple picks the closest match first and lets you switch with one click. Anything you typed yourself stays put when you switch.

Requirements

A Mac
With Apple silicon, running macOS 26 or later.
A disc drive
Most Macs have no disc drive. A USB CD or DVD drive works.
Internet
Only for the album and cover lookup. You can type the details yourself without it.
